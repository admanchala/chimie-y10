Exam-style questions

Longer questions worth several marks, just like in a test. Write a full answer before opening the markscheme.

📝 Method: as a rule, 1 mark = 1 scientific idea. For a 4-mark question, your answer should contain 4 distinct, logically connected statements — not a single vague sentence.
Kinetics · E1[4 marks]
Using collision theory, explain why the reaction between magnesium and hydrochloric acid is faster at 40 °C than at 20 °C.
Markscheme
  • At 40 °C, the particles have a higher average kinetic energy (1)
  • They move faster → collisions between the H⁺ ions and the Mg atoms are more frequent (1)
  • A greater proportion of particles have energy ≥ Eₐ (1)
  • The proportion of successful collisions increases → the rate of reaction is higher (1)
Kinetics · E2[6 marks]
A student studies the reaction between calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid by measuring the volume of CO₂ released over time.
(a) Describe how the rate of reaction changes over time and explain why. [3]
(b) The experiment is repeated with more concentrated HCl (same volume, excess CaCO₃). Describe the shape of the new curve compared with the first: initial gradient and final volume of CO₂. Justify. [3]
Markscheme
  • (a) The rate is greatest at the start (steepest gradient) (1)
  • (a) It decreases over time because the HCl concentration decreases → fewer collisions (1)
  • (a) The curve becomes horizontal: the reaction is finished (limiting reactant used up) (1)
  • (b) Steeper initial gradient: more H⁺ ions per unit volume → more frequent collisions (1)
  • (b) The reaction finishes sooner (1)
  • (b) Larger final volume of CO₂ because more moles of HCl have reacted (HCl limiting) (1)
Redox · E1[4 marks]
A zinc strip is placed in a solution of silver nitrate (AgNO₃). Describe what is observed and explain the reaction using the activity series and half-equations.
Markscheme
  • Observation: a grey/silvery deposit forms on the zinc strip (metallic silver) (1)
  • Zn is more reactive than Ag → Zn displaces Ag⁺ from its salt solution (1)
  • Oxidation: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (1)
  • Reduction: 2 Ag⁺ + 2e⁻ → 2 Ag (1)
Redox · E2[5 marks]
A voltaic cell is built with a magnesium electrode and a copper electrode connected by a salt bridge.
(a) Identify the anode and the cathode. [1]
(b) Write the two half-equations. [2]
(c) State the direction of electron flow. [1]
(d) Predict whether the voltage will be higher or lower than a Zn/Cu cell. Justify. [1]
Markscheme
  • (a) Anode (−): Mg (the more reactive); Cathode (+): Cu (1)
  • (b) Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ (1)
  • (b) Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (1)
  • (c) The electrons flow from the anode (Mg) to the cathode (Cu) through the external circuit (1)
  • (d) Higher voltage: the Mg/Cu reactivity difference is greater than Zn/Cu (≈ 2.7 V vs 1.1 V) (1)
Acids & bases · E1[4 marks]
At equal concentration (0.1 mol/L), ethanoic acid reacts more slowly with magnesium than hydrochloric acid. Explain why, using the ideas of strong/weak acids and collision theory.
Markscheme
  • Ethanoic acid is a weak acid: it dissociates partially into ions (1)
  • HCl is a strong acid: complete dissociation → higher [H⁺] (1)
  • With ethanoic acid, the H⁺ concentration is lower → less frequent H⁺/Mg collisions (1)
  • Lower frequency of successful collisions → slower rate of reaction (1)
Acids & bases · E2[6 marks]
A student titrates 25.0 cm³ of an HCl solution of unknown concentration with 0.150 mol/L NaOH. The equivalence point is reached after adding 22.0 cm³ of NaOH.
(a) Write the balanced equation for the reaction. [1]
(b) Calculate the number of moles of NaOH added. [1]
(c) Deduce the number of moles of HCl. [1]
(d) Calculate the concentration of the HCl. [2]
(e) Name a suitable indicator and the colour change observed at the equivalence point. [1]
Markscheme
  • (a) HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (1)
  • (b) n(NaOH) = 0.150 × 0.0220 = 3.30 × 10⁻³ mol (1)
  • (c) 1:1 reaction → n(HCl) = 3.30 × 10⁻³ mol (1)
  • (d) C = n ÷ V = 3.30 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 (1) = 0.132 mol/L (1)
  • (e) E.g. phenolphthalein: colourless → pink (adding base to acid); any indicator with a sharp colour change accepted (1)
