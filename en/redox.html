2.1Oxidation and reduction

Key definition

Oxidation: loss of electrons (the species is oxidised).

Key definition

Reduction: gain of electrons (the species is reduced).

OIL RIG Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons) · Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).
In every redox reaction, oxidation and reduction happen simultaneously: the electrons lost by one reactant are gained by the other.

2.2Activity series of metals

More reactive →  K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au  ← Less reactive

Links with the periodic table

Group 1 metals (Li, Na, K) are very reactive because they easily lose their single valence electron. Group 2 metals (Mg, Ca) are less reactive than Group 1 but more reactive than most transition metals. Going down a group, reactivity increases: the valence electron is further from the nucleus, so it is easier to lose.

2.3Displacement reactions & half-equations

A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its salt solution. Rule: if metal A is more reactive than metal B, then A can displace B from a solution of B's salt.

Example: zinc + copper(II) sulfate

Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

Oxidation (Zn loses electrons):

Zn(s) → Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻

Reduction (Cu²⁺ gains electrons):

Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)

Method for writing a half-equation

1. Write the formula of the metal or ion on both sides.  2. Balance the charges by adding electrons (e⁻) on the correct side.  3. Check that both mass AND charge are balanced.

2.4Extraction of metals

ReactivityExtraction method
Very reactive (K, Na, Mg, Al)Electrolysis (requires a lot of energy)
Medium reactivity (Zn, Fe)Reduction by carbon (coke) in a blast furnace
Low reactivity (Cu, Pb)Reduction by carbon or simple heating
Very low reactivity (Ag, Au, Pt)Found native (pure metal in nature)

2.5Heat of reaction & calorimetry

Some displacement reactions are exothermic. The heat released is calculated with:

q = m × C × ΔT

q = quantity of heat (J) · m = mass of the solution (g) · C = specific heat capacity of water = 4.18 J·g⁻¹·°C⁻¹ · ΔT = Tfinal − Tinitial (°C)

Interpretation: the greater the difference in reactivity between two metals, the more exothermic the reaction, and therefore the higher ΔT.

2.6Voltaic cells

A voltaic cell (or galvanic cell) converts the chemical energy of a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy.

ComponentRole
Anode (−)The more reactive metal — it is oxidised (loses electrons)
Cathode (+)The less reactive metal — it is reduced (gains electrons)
Salt bridgeAllows ions to pass through to balance the charges
External circuitElectrons flow from the anode to the cathode

Example — the Daniell cell (Zn/Cu)

Anode: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation) · Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (reduction) · Voltage ≈ 1.1 V

Deducing reactivity from voltage: a higher voltage indicates a greater difference in reactivity between the two metals. By comparing the voltages of different cells, the relative reactivity order of metals can be established.

2.7Metallic and ionic bonding

Metallic bonding

In a metal, the atoms lose their valence electrons to form positive ions (cations) surrounded by a "sea" of delocalised electrons. This structure explains the electrical and thermal conductivity of metals.

Ionic bonding

A metal gives electrons to a non-metal to form ions of opposite charges, which attract each other.

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ then Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ → NaCl (table salt)

2.8Worked examples

Example 1Displacement reaction — iron in CuSO₄▾

Question: iron is placed in a solution of copper(II) sulfate. Write the overall equation and the two half-equations. Identify the species oxidised and the species reduced.

Answer Iron is more reactive than copper → displacement is possible.
Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)
Fe(s) → Fe²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻  (oxidation)
Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)  (reduction)
Iron is oxidised; the copper ion is reduced.
Example 2Calorimetry calculation — zinc + CuSO₄▾

Question: 50 cm³ of 0.5 mol/L copper(II) sulfate solution is placed in a calorimeter. An excess of zinc powder is added. The temperature rises from 21.0 °C to 28.4 °C. Calculate the heat released. (Density ≈ 1 g/cm³)

Answer m = 50 g · ΔT = 28.4 − 21.0 = 7.4 °C · C = 4.18 J·g⁻¹·°C⁻¹
q = mCΔT = 50 × 4.18 × 7.4 = 1546.6 J ≈ 1.55 kJ
The reaction is exothermic (positive ΔT).
Example 3Voltaic cell Mg/Cu vs Zn/Cu▾

Question: a cell is built with magnesium and copper. Identify the anode and the cathode, write the half-equations and predict whether the voltage will be higher or lower than a Zn/Cu cell.

Answer Mg is more reactive than Cu → Mg is the anode (oxidised); Cu is the cathode (reduced).
Mg(s) → Mg²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻
Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)
The difference in reactivity between Mg and Cu is greater than between Zn and Cu → the voltage will be higher than that of the Zn/Cu cell (≈ 2.7 V vs 1.1 V). Check it with the cell builder above!
