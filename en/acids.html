Acids & bases

Donating or accepting a proton: from the pH of vinegar to precision titration, this is the chemistry of acids and bases.

3.1The Brønsted-Lowry definition

Key definition

Acid (Brønsted-Lowry): a chemical species able to donate a proton (H⁺) to another species.

Key definition

Base (Brønsted-Lowry): a chemical species able to accept a proton (H⁺).

This definition is broader than the classical one: it applies even to non-aqueous solvents and insoluble bases.

HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻

HCl donates an H⁺ to water → HCl is the acid; water accepts the H⁺ → water is the base.

3.2Alkalis and insoluble bases

Alkalis (water-soluble bases)

They dissolve in water to produce OH⁻ ions:

NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻
Ca(OH)₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2 OH⁻
NH₃ + H₂O → NH₄⁺ + OH⁻

Insoluble bases

They do not dissolve in water but can still accept protons:

ZnO (zinc oxide) · Fe₂O₃ (iron oxide) · CaCO₃ (calcium carbonate)

All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis.

3.4Strong vs weak

Strong acid: dissociates completely into ions in water — HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃. · Weak acid: dissociates partially — CH₃COOH (ethanoic acid). · Strong base: NaOH. · Weak base: NH₃.

⚠️ Careful: the strength of an acid (strong/weak) is different from its concentration (molarity).

3.5pH and H⁺ concentration

The pH measures the concentration of H⁺ (or H₃O⁺) ions in solution: pH < 7 → acidic · pH = 7 → neutral (at 25 °C) · pH > 7 → basic.

Key relationship: the pH decreases by one unit for every tenfold increase in H⁺ concentration. (Formally: pH = −log[H⁺], but knowledge of logarithms is not required at this level.)

Universal indicator: the only indicator able to distinguish different pH values across the whole scale (0–14) thanks to a gradual colour change (red → violet).

3.6Neutralisation and titration

Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a base to produce a salt and water.

HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

At the equivalence point, the number of moles of acid = the number of moles of base (according to the stoichiometry). Titration is used to determine the concentration of an unknown solution:

n(acid) = C₁ × V₁   and   n(base) = C₂ × V₂   →   at the equivalence point (1:1): C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

Method

1. Fill the burette with the solution of known concentration.  2. Put the unknown solution in the conical flask with a few drops of indicator.  3. Add slowly until the indicator changes colour (equivalence point).  4. Read the volume added and calculate the unknown concentration.

3.7Enthalpy of neutralisation

Neutralisation is an exothermic reaction. The heat released is calculated with q = mCΔT, and the molar enthalpy of neutralisation:

ΔH(neut) = −q ÷ n   (in kJ/mol)

Where n is the number of moles of the limiting reactant (acid or base).

Reference value: for the neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong base, ΔH ≈ −57 kJ/mol. 💡 The q = mCΔT calculator on the Redox page works here too!

3.8Worked examples

Example 1The three characteristic reactions of acids▾

Question: write the balanced equations for: (a) magnesium + hydrochloric acid; (b) sodium hydroxide + sulfuric acid; (c) calcium carbonate + hydrochloric acid.

Answer
(a) Mg(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
(b) 2 NaOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2 H₂O(l)
(c) CaCO₃(s) + 2 HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)
Example 2Titration — concentration of NaOH▾

Question: in a titration, 25.0 cm³ of NaOH of unknown concentration is titrated with 0.100 mol/L HCl. 18.4 cm³ of HCl is needed to reach the equivalence point. Calculate the concentration of the NaOH.

Answer Moles of HCl = 0.100 × 0.0184 = 1.84 × 10⁻³ mol. 1:1 reaction, so moles of NaOH = 1.84 × 10⁻³ mol.
C(NaOH) = n ÷ V = 1.84 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0736 mol/L
Example 3Enthalpy of neutralisation HCl + NaOH▾

Question: 50 cm³ of 1 mol/L HCl is mixed with 50 cm³ of 1 mol/L NaOH. The temperature rises from 20.0 °C to 26.7 °C. Calculate q and the molar ΔH of neutralisation.

Answer Total m = 100 g (density ≈ 1 g/cm³) · ΔT = 6.7 °C
q = 100 × 4.18 × 6.7 = 2800.6 J ≈ 2.80 kJ
Moles of HCl = 0.050 L × 1 mol/L = 0.050 mol (limiting reactant)
ΔH = −2.80 ÷ 0.050 = −56.0 kJ/mol
The negative sign confirms that the reaction is exothermic.
